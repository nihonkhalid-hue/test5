# Additional US Marketplaces and Free-Traffic Platforms for a Foreign-Owned US LLC (Moroccan Home Decor + Retro/Nostalgia), as of Oct 2026

Seller context: US single-member LLC owned by a Moroccan resident, with an EIN, a Mercury US bank account and inventory at a 3PL in Texas. The owner logs in from Morocco, has no SSN and may get an ITIN.

Scope: platforms other than Amazon, Walmart, Etsy, eBay, Shopify, TikTok Shop and Facebook. About 27 searches were run; many official help pages could not be fetched (for example, help.whatnot.com did not resolve). A lot of the fee data comes from third-party guides and is flagged that way.

## 1. Free / organic traffic channels (feed-based, no commission)

### Takeaway
The best no-commission traffic sources for a US-registered LLC with its own Shopify-style site are Google free listings (Merchant Center), Pinterest product pins and the Verified Merchant Program, and YouTube Shopping. All three are feed-based and send buyers to the seller's own checkout. Instagram native checkout is no longer a dependable option in the US.

### Cited Findings
- **Google free listings:** the shop must "sell in a supported country or region" for synced products to show in free listings across Google. The return policy should be on the website, and shipping should be set up in Merchant Center. The US is a supported shipping-attribute country. — [Google Merchant Center Help: Free listings](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13889434)
- **Google free listings:** in 2020 Google said merchants "will no longer have to be a U.S. retailer" to use free product listings. — [Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/google-expands-free-product-listings-globally); see also [Google help](https://pss.support.google.com/merchants/answer/10033607)
- **Google free listings:** having free-listing status gives permission to show products but does not guarantee impressions. — [Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13889434)
- **Pinterest Verified Merchant Program (VMP):** a Pinterest community FAQ lists the available countries as Australia, Austria, Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Mexico, the Netherlands, Spain, Switzerland, the UK and the US. Morocco is not on the list. — [Pinterest Business Community FAQ](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/verified-merchant-program-workshop-faq-recap/842); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/pinterest-adds-new-shopping-features-including-brand-profile-details-and-s/607798)
- **Pinterest VMP requirements:**
  - a connected product catalog, the Pinterest tag installed, and compliance with the Merchant Guidelines
  - merchants must not be resale/consignment marketplaces or affiliate marketers — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/pinterest-launches-verified-merchant-program-with-applications-open-to-all/357466/)
  - at least 50% of the catalog must ingest successfully; inventory size does not matter; Shopify and WooCommerce make setup easier — [Pinterest community FAQ](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/verified-merchant-program-workshop-faq-recap/842)
- **Pinterest VMP rejections:** a moderator gave possible reasons for one rejection: a marketplace-style layout, stock imagery and a Gmail contact address. The moderator could not confirm these. — [Pinterest community](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/merchant-does-not-meet-minimum-website-quality/1144)
- **Pinterest VMP outside listed countries:** a merchant in India reported being stuck "In Review" when applying. — [Pinterest community](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/unable-to-apply-for-pinterest-merchant-program-from-india/42302)
- **YouTube Shopping affiliate program, creator side:** creators must be in the YouTube Partner Program, and the channel must be based in an eligible country (US, Korea, India, Brazil, Japan and others). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13376398?hl=en)
- **YouTube Shopping, merchant side (Shopify):**
  - merchants must not use headless Shopify and must follow Merchant Center guidelines
  - merchants pay only when creators drive a sale, and checkout stays on the merchant's own website — [Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13773425?hl=en)
- **Instagram:** from April 24, 2024, US shops without checkout on Facebook and Instagram enabled are no longer supported, and checkout requires a website the seller owns. — [Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/iphone-app/951629962247940)
- **Instagram Live:** Meta discontinued product tagging during Live in March 2023. — [Makeinfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/live-shopping-beyond-tiktok-whatnot-amazon-live-and-instagram)

### Inferences
- **Pinterest:** the US LLC (US address, US bank) should apply for Pinterest VMP as a US merchant. If Pinterest checks the account country, it should be US. Morocco is not a listed VMP country, so the account should not be registered as Moroccan.
- **Instagram:** treat it as an organic content and brand channel that links out to the site, not as a sales channel.
- **Google:** free listings are low-effort and mostly no-cost once a Shopify site exists. The "Moroccan rug" and "Berber rug" product queries are a natural fit.
- **YouTube Shopping:** it is mainly useful for recruiting US home-decor creators as affiliates. The owner's own channel would need to be US-based and in the Partner Program.
- **Pinterest fit:** Pinterest is historically strong for home decor inspiration (rugs, poufs, lamps).

### Gaps
- No 2026-dated official statement was found on whether a US LLC with a foreign owner who logs in from Morocco triggers extra verification on Google, Pinterest or YouTube.
- No Pinterest or Google traffic figures for these product categories were found.

## 2. Home decor / furniture marketplaces

### Takeaway
- **Shop Houzz:** shut down in May 2026, so it is no longer an option.
- **Chairish and 1stDibs:** curated and high-commission (20–40%), with modest traffic (Chairish about 2M visits/month). They suit vintage or higher-end rugs and lamps.
- **Wayfair:** a supplier (wholesale/dropship) program with heavy EDI/GS1 requirements, not an open marketplace.
- **AptDeco:** used and local furniture; sellers keep up to 70%.

### Cited Findings
- **Shop Houzz:**
  - stopped taking orders on May 7, 2026 and was set to close on May 22, 2026, per a notice to vendors — [Business of Home, May 19, 2026](https://businessofhome.com/articles/houzz-s-shopping-site-winds-down-cohen-defaults-on-d-d-building-mortgage-and-more); [Home News Now](https://homenewsnow.com/?p=70089)
  - it had been separated in 2025 and was operated by Cart.com Holdings 2 LLC; the Houzz.com and Houzz Pro platforms are unaffected — [Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/houzz-s-shopping-site-winds-down-cohen-defaults-on-d-d-building-mortgage-and-more)
- **Chairish commission (official help pages):**
  - Consignor plan: 40%; Professional plan: 30%
  - Platinum/Elite tiers: sliding scale of 20% up to $2,500, 12% from $2,501 to $25,000, and 3% above $25,000
  - Platinum costs $200/month and includes 0% commission on Chairish Auctions (powered by LiveAuctioneers) — [Chairish support](https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/115006460407-Chairish-Selling-Plans-Commission-Rate-O)
  - a blog's claim of a flat 30% conflicts with the official page — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/chairish-selling-guide-furniture-2026)
- **Chairish traffic:** Semrush estimates about 2.41M visits in September 2025 and about 2.02M in October 2025. — [Semrush](https://cw.semrush.com/website/chairish.com/overview)
- **1stDibs:**
  - curated, with an application that requires references and proof of being an established seller — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/1stdibs-selling-guide-antiques-2026)
  - the no-subscription option ended in 2024 — [yespress.io](https://yespress.io/1stdibs)
  - commission is about 15–30% depending on plan, plus about 3% processing (third-party, unofficial) — [estimonia](https://estimonia.com/directory/1stdibs)
  - about 1.9M listings from 5,700 sellers as of December 2025, per the 10-K summary — [yespress / underpriced, citing 10-K](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/1stdibs-selling-guide-antiques-2026)
- **Wayfair (supplier program):**
  - drop-ship model where the supplier ships to the customer
  - the supplier must meet inventory-accuracy, lead-time and packaging standards — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/how-suppliers-join-the-wayfair-drop-ship-program/)
  - EDI transaction sets 850/856/810/846, GS1-128 labels and GS1 GTINs are required, with Net 30–60 payment terms — [Endless Commerce](https://endlesscommerce.com/edi/requirements/wayfair/)
  - CastleGate Multichannel (3PL for big and bulky goods) was opened to all suppliers — [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/wayfair-castlegate-logistics-offering-multichannel-service/758661/)
- **AptDeco:**
  - sellers "keep up to 70%" of the sale price
  - Pro Seller (5 or more pieces a month) gets reduced fees — [AptDeco sell page](https://www.aptdeco.com/sell); [AptDeco Pro](https://www.aptdeco.com/pro-sellers)

### Inferences
- **Wayfair:** Wayfair buys wholesale and does not take Etsy-style listings. A small artisan importer can apply through the Partner Home portal, but the EDI/GS1 overhead and Wayfair-set retail pricing make it a medium-term option. A Texas 3PL that supports EDI is needed.
- **Chairish:** a fit for authentic vintage Moroccan rugs (Beni Ourain, Boucherouite) and vintage lamps. The 20–40% commission needs high-AOV pricing.
- **AptDeco and Kaiyo:** they focus on used furniture with local white-glove pickup (NYC and similar areas). They are a poor fit for new imported inventory held in Texas.

### Gaps
- No Kaiyo fee or eligibility data was found.
- No official Wayfair supplier fee document for 2026 was found.
- No 2026 traffic data was found for 1stDibs, Wayfair or Houzz.
- No source confirmed whether Houzz.com still runs any product marketplace after the Shop Houzz shutdown.

## 3. Handmade / artisan / gift marketplaces

### Takeaway
- **Amazon Handmade:** a strong fit (15% referral fee, Professional plan fee waived when approved). It requires a maker audit, and an importer/reseller of others' work may not qualify unless the owner's workshop or artisans are presented properly.
- **Michaels MakerPlace:** low fees but small traffic; one seller reported a fee change.
- **NOVICA:** an artisan-sourced model (it buys from artisans), not a seller marketplace for a US LLC.
- **Uncommon Goods:** wholesale submission only.

### Cited Findings
- **Amazon Handmade fees:** 15% referral fee with no listing or processing fees. The $39.99/month Professional fee is waived for approved applications. — [Amazon Handmade](https://services.amazon.com/handmade)
- **Amazon Handmade application:**
  - an application with an audit to confirm the goods are handcrafted; artisans from more than 80 countries sell there — [Amazon.ca Handmade](https://sell.amazon.ca/programs/handmade)
  - can take up to two weeks — [Viral Launch](https://viral-launch.com/?p=109772)
- **Michaels MakerPlace fees:** no listing fee. The referral fee is 4% (Basic) or 2% (Pro at $9.98/month), plus 3% + $0.20 per transaction. — [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/etsy-gets-a-new-competitor-focused-on-original-arts-and-crafts-110623.html); [Sellers Ask Sellers](https://sellersasksellers.com/t/fees-on-orders/2991)
- **Michaels MakerPlace 2025 claim:** a seller-forum post said Michaels would charge a $110 professional fee from January 2025. This is not officially confirmed, and some sellers in the thread said they were closing their accounts. — [Sellers Ask Sellers](https://sellersasksellers.com/t/michaels-maker-place-will-charge-the-professional-fee-in-jan-2025/5313)
- **NOVICA:** artisans set their own prices, and NOVICA handles transport to its regional hubs and supports artisans with zero-interest loans. It serves more than 21,000 artisans; Morocco was not named in these sources. — [Global Partnerships PDF](https://globalpartnerships.org/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/all/IFDF-Partner-Profile-Q2-2023-Novica.pdf); [NOVICA mission](https://novica.com/en-ca/our-mission/)
- **Uncommon Goods:**
  - accepts product submissions and partnership inquiries; vendors must meet quality, originality and brand-alignment standards and provide product safety certificates (GCC) — [Uncommon Goods support](https://support.uncommongoods.com/hc/en-us/articles/49416395755547-How-do-I-partner-with-Uncommon-Goods)
  - dropship only from a select group of makers who ship directly; it does not sell to resellers — [Uncommon Goods vendor](https://vendor.uncommongoods.com/?cat=25)

### Inferences
- **Amazon Handmade:** the best fit is poufs, leather bags, ceramics and textiles where the owner can document the artisan or workshop. It still lives inside Amazon Seller Central, which is excluded from this list but is a separate program.
- **Michaels MakerPlace:** low priority given the small audience and uncertain fees.
- **NOVICA:** could become a wholesale buyer of Moroccan artisan products if NOVICA sources in Morocco (unconfirmed). It is not a channel the LLC can self-list on.
- **Uncommon Goods:** a wholesale pitch for distinctive items such as hand-painted ceramics or leather goods.

### Gaps
- Michaels MakerPlace's status and fees as of October 2026 were not confirmed.
- NOVICA's Morocco coverage was not confirmed.
- No Zola Shop vendor program data was found (Zola is mainly a registry).
- No Catch data was found; Catch is an Australian marketplace and probably not relevant to the US.

## 4. Vintage / retro / resale marketplaces (retro-nostalgia line)

### Takeaway
- **Depop:** cheapest (0% commission in the US).
- **Mercari:** about 10%, with large traffic (about 100M visits/month).
- **Poshmark:** 20%.
- **Whatnot:** about 8% + 2.9% + $0.30, and live auctions suit retro and collectibles.
- **Ruby Lane:** $45/month plus a 9.9% tiered fee, aimed at antiques.
- **Etsy Vintage:** requires items 20+ years old (made in 2006 or earlier in 2026).
- **Bonanza:** commission rose to 11% in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Mercari:** about 10% seller fee; the processing fee was dropped in January 2025. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/mercari-fees-explained-complete-guide-2026); [Nifty](https://www.nifty.ai/post/mercari-fees)
- **Poshmark:** 20% on sales of $15 or more; flat $2.95 under $15. — [Nifty comparison](https://nifty.ai/post/online-selling-fees-comparison)
- **Depop:** US commission is 0%; processing is 3.3% + $0.45; optional Boost is 12%. — [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/fees/the-real-cost-of-business-breaking-down-depop-selling-fees-in-2025)
- **Mercari traffic:** about 100.23M visits (Semrush, July 2025). — [Semrush](https://ja.semrush.com/website/poshmark.com/competitors)
- **Whatnot fees:**
  - about 8% commission on item price plus 2.9% + $0.30 processing, with no listing or monthly fees — [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/fees/whatnot-selling-fees); [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/whatnot-seller-fees-2026)
  - some sources list category-based rates of 4–8% — [Size.ly](https://www.size.ly/tools/whatnot-fee-calculator)
- **Whatnot eligibility:** a third-party blog lists eligible seller locations as the US, Canada, UK, France, Germany, Ireland and select EU countries. The payout account must be a US or supported-country bank; PayPal and Venmo are not accepted. Sellers need government ID and must be 18 or older. — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/how-to-get-approved-to-sell-on-whatnot-2026)
- **Whatnot EU sellers:** EU sellers must be VAT-registered, per a tracker quoting Whatnot's terms. — [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/whatnot/whatnot-terms-of-service/provision/CA-P-062328/eu-terms-eu-seller-vat-registration-required/)
- **Ruby Lane:**
  - no listing fees; $45/month shop maintenance; service fee of 9.9% on the first $2,500, 5% from $2,500 to $7,500, and 2.5% above that — [unanswered.io](https://unanswered.io/guide/ruby-lane-selling-fees)
  - EcommerceBytes reported the increase from $25 to $45/month — [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/tag/ruby-lane-fees/)
  - a conflicting figure of $54/month plus 6.7% appears — [Underpriced](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/rubylane-selling-guide-2026)
- **Etsy Vintage:** items must be 20+ years old ("When Made" more than 20 years before the current date). — [Listadum](https://www.listadum.com/blog/how-to-sell-vintage-on-etsy); [ExportFeed](https://exportfeed.helpscoutdocs.com/article/29-error-115-oh-dear-you-cannot-sell-this-item-on-etsy)
- **Bonanza:**
  - commission rose from 3.5% to 11% effective May 1, 2025, with new fees for larger booths and $0.25 per transaction without a membership — [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/tag/bonanza-fees/)
  - a 2026-titled article still cites 3.5%, which is likely outdated — [Valigara](https://www.valigara.com/jewelry-ecommerce-articles/is-selling-on-bonanza-com-still-worth-it-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Retro-style products:** new "retro" or nostalgia items are not vintage. They cannot go in Etsy Vintage, Ruby Lane or Chairish vintage. They fit Mercari, Depop and Whatnot (live), and Etsy only as craft supplies or handmade.
- **Whatnot:** because the selling entity is a US LLC with a US bank, the seller is plausibly "US-based" for Whatnot purposes. Live shows hosted from Morocco while the 3PL ships from Texas are workable in principle. Whatnot's verification of a foreign individual's ID was not confirmed.
- **Poshmark (home category):** 20% is costly. Mercari's large traffic and 10% fee are a better fit for poufs and leather bags.
- **Depop:** skews young and fashion; leather bags fit best.

### Gaps
- No official Whatnot statement was found on foreign-owner eligibility (the help site could not be fetched).
- No Poshmark, Depop or Whatnot traffic data was found.
- No OfferUp, Nextdoor or Craigslist research was done. These are local pickup channels that do not fit a Texas 3PL without local presence (inference).

## 5. Big-box retailer marketplaces (Target Plus, Kohl's/Macy's, Overstock/BB&B, Zulily, Temu local)

### Takeaway
These are invite-only or vendor programs, or have an uncertain status. They are low-probability options for a new foreign-owned micro LLC.

### Cited Findings
- **Target Plus:**
  - invite-only, with a US business and bank presence, price parity, shipping within 24 hours with transit of 5 days or less, and US-only shipping — [ChannelEngine](https://www.channelengine.com/en/blog/selling-on-target-plus?hsLang=en); [Feedonomics](https://feedonomics.com/blog/selling-on-target/)
  - liability insurance and safety test reports — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/how-to-become-a-target-plus-marketplace-seller-steps-requirements-and-tips/)
- **Zulily:**
  - Beyond sold 75% to Lyons Trading Co. (Proozy) for $5M in March 2025, with a relaunch planned and no date set — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/zulily-goes-through-another-ownership-change-as-beyond-to-sell-majority-stake-in-retail-site-for-5m/); [Total Retail](https://mytotalretail.com/article/beyond-sells-majority-stake-in-zulily-for-5-million)
  - one report says Zulily shut down after only 7 months — [Cleverence](https://www.cleverence.com/amp/articles/business-blogs/online-e-commerceer-zulily-shuts-down-after-only-7-months/)
- **Overstock / Bed Bath & Beyond:** both now operate under Bed Bath & Beyond, Inc. (formerly Beyond, Inc.). There is no clear public third-party seller program; sources are low-authority. — [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/who-owns-overstock-and-wayfair)
- **Temu Local Seller Program:**
  - launched in the US in March 2024 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temu)
  - active in 37 markets per a 2026 sponsored article — [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/04/30/sell-locally-and-now-into-regional-markets-temus-local-seller-program-explained-2026-update/)
  - required documents depend on "seller type, location, ownership structure" — [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/temu-seller-center-setup-and-selling-guide-for-2026)

### Inferences
- **Temu:** it is the most accessible of this group (open application for US-stocked inventory). Its price-driven audience conflicts with premium handmade pricing, but cheap retro novelty items may fit.
- **Target Plus, Kohl's and Macy's:** treat them as wholesale/brand targets for later.

### Gaps
- Kohl's and Macy's Mirakl marketplace details were not found.
- Zulily's relaunch status as of October 2026 is unclear.
- No Temu foreign-UBO rules were found.

## 6. Cross-border and wholesale (Fruugo, Joom, Faire)

### Takeaway
- **Fruugo:** open to US-registered businesses and charges about 20% all-in.
- **Faire:** wholesale with organic retailer discovery. The commission is 15% on reorders and about 25% on first orders, with an extra 10% for non-North-American brands, which a US LLC likely avoids.

### Cited Findings
- **Fruugo fees:**
  - a single fee of about 20% of order value covering commission, processing and marketing — [Fruugo sell page](https://sell.fruugo.com/?p=2964)
  - older docs say 15% + 2.35% — [Fruugo wiki](https://fruugo.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/RR/pages/55148567/Commission)
- **Fruugo eligibility:** sellers must be registered in one of the supported countries, which include the US and Canada. — [Volo](https://www.volocommerce.com/explore/blog/exploring-the-fruugo-marketplace/)
- **Faire fees:**
  - 25% on the first order from a new retailer, 15% on reorders and 0% via Faire Direct; one source says $10 per order
  - brands outside North America pay an extra 10% on first orders — [Orosy](https://wholesale-portal.orosy.com/en/blog/faire-japanese-brands-vs-sourcing-direct-from-japan/); [Faire support](https://www.faire.com/en/support/articles/29090131049371)

### Inferences
- **Faire:** a strong fit for Moroccan poufs, ceramics and textiles sold to US boutiques, and it can ship from the Texas 3PL. The surcharge for brands outside North America likely does not apply to a US LLC with US inventory (verify).
- **Fruugo:** mainly sends US stock to international buyers, which means high shipping costs from Texas. It has limited value.

### Gaps
- No Joom seller data was found.
- No traffic figures were found for Fruugo or Faire.
