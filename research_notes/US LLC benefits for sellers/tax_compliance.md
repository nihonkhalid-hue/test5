## 1. Form 5472 + pro forma Form 1120 (and when Form 1040-NR applies)

### Takeaway
Every year the LLC exists (even with zero activity), a foreign-owned single-member LLC must file a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached by April 15 (extendable to Oct 15 via Form 7004). It cannot be e-filed; it goes by fax or mail to a special IRS unit. The penalty is $25,000 per form per year, plus $25,000 for each further 30 days of non-filing after an IRS notice. Separately, Form 1040-NR is needed only if the owner has US-taxable income (ECI or under-withheld US-source FDAP).

### Cited Findings
- A foreign-owned SMLLC must file a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached even with zero income or tax. The 1120 is filed "solely for the purpose of attaching Form 5472" — [Taxes for Expats](https://www.taxesforexpats.com/articles/foreign-business/form-5472-penalty.html); [Nonresident Tax guide](https://nonresident.tax/blog/form-5472-pro-forma-1120-guide-nonresident-owners/)
- It is filed even with no US-source income, no worldwide income, or no activity in the year — [Taxes for Expats Form 5472 article](https://cdn.taxesforexpats.com/articles/foreign-business/form-5472.html); [GlobalSolo](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/what-happens-if-you-miss-form-5472-non-resident-llc)
- Deadline: April 15 for calendar-year filers, extendable to October 15 by filing Form 7004 by the original due date. On Form 7004, enter the Form 1120 code in Part I line 1 and write "Foreign-Owned U.S. DE" across the top — [Kozlaw](https://kozlaw.com/april-15-deadline-u-s-form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/); [Hiltzik CPA](https://hiltzikcpa.com/form-5472-filing-guide/)
  - Conflict: one secondary source gives March 15. That is inconsistent with the Form 1120 due date. April 15 is the consensus — [Slash](https://www.slash.com/tax-forms/5472) vs. the sources above.
- Filing method: e-filing is not accepted for these returns. Secondary sources give fax 855-887-7737, or mail to the IRS in Ogden, UT, "Attn: PIN Unit" (per the December 2024 instructions). I could not open the IRS instructions to verify the fax number — [Globalfy](https://globalfy.com/blog/form-5472/); [Greenback](https://www.greenbacktaxservices.com/knowledge-center/form-5472/); [Finbryn](https://finbryn.com/us/guides/form-5472-foreign-owned-llc-guide)
- Penalty: $25,000 per form per year under IRC §6038A(d). If the failure continues more than 90 days after an IRS notice, a further $25,000 applies for each 30-day period or part of one. One source says "90-day periods", but the 30-day reading matches the statute — [Taxes for Expats](https://www.taxesforexpats.com/articles/foreign-business/form-5472-penalty.html); [SDO CPA](https://www.sdocpa.com/form-5472-penalties-relief-guide/)
- Reportable transactions include contributions and distributions between the owner and the LLC. Capital put in by the owner, owner draws, and LLC-paid expenses for the owner are the typical "related-party transactions" for a DE — [Nonresident Tax](https://nonresident.tax/blog/form-5472-pro-forma-1120-guide-nonresident-owners/); [Jupid](https://jupid.com/blog/form-5472-foreign-owned-llc-2026)
- 1040-NR: if income is ECI, the owner files Form 1040-NR. If not, the LLC files only the pro forma 1120/5472 — [Entity.inc](https://www.entity.inc/blog/effectively-connected-income-eci/); [Cheq Capital](https://cheqcapital.com/en/us-llc-tax-non-residents/)
- Penalty-assessment litigation: the Tax Court held in Farhy (2023) and Mukhi (2024, 162 T.C. No. 8) that the IRS cannot summarily assess §6038(b) (Form 5471) penalties. The D.C. Circuit reversed Farhy on May 3, 2024. Mukhi is appealable to the 8th Circuit, so a circuit split was possible — [Marcum](https://marcumllp.com/insights/dc-circuit-lets-stand-its-decision-allowing-irs-to-assess-international-information-return-penalties); [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2024/tax-court-reaffirms-irs-lack-of-authority-to-assess-form-5471-penalties.html). A separate case report says Form 5472 penalties were "properly assessed" — [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/high-net-worth/penalties-properly-assessed-for-failure-to-file-form-5472)

### Inferences
- For an LLC about 1.5 years old: if it was formed in 2025 (or late 2024), at least one 5472/1120 filing (tax year 2025, due Apr 15 2026 or Oct 15 2026 if extended) should already have been made. If it was formed in 2024, the 2024 return (due Apr 15 2025) was also required. Today, 2026-10-10, is 5 days before the extended Oct 15 2026 deadline for tax year 2025. This is urgent if no extension or return has been filed.
- Do not rely on Farhy/Mukhi to escape 5472 penalties. §6038A is a different section, the D.C. Circuit sided with the IRS, and I found no confirmed 8th Circuit result.
- Recommended filing practice (common practitioner advice): fax and keep the fax confirmation, or mail by tracked courier. Keep a copy plus proof of timely filing.

### Gaps
- Could not read the current IRS Form 5472 instructions (Rev. Dec 2024 or later) to confirm the fax number and the full Ogden mailing address. The commonly cited address, from memory and not verified here, is "Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd., M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201".
- Whether the 8th Circuit decided the Mukhi appeal in 2025/2026: not found.

## 2. ECI / US trade or business (USTB) analysis and the US–Morocco treaty

### Takeaway
A Morocco-resident owner with no US office, employees or dependent agent, who ships from Morocco, is generally treated by practitioners as not engaged in a USTB. US federal income tax is then $0, and only 5472/1120 is filed. Holding your own inventory in a US 3PL or warehouse (or Amazon FBA) is the main gray zone, and advisors disagree. The 1977 US–Morocco treaty (in force 30 Dec 1981) puts PE rules in Article 4 and business profits in Article 7. It can shield business profits without a US PE, but I could not verify the exact PE exclusions in the US text.

### Cited Findings
- Practitioner position: a foreign-owned LLC with no US office, staff or dependent agent is usually not engaged in a USTB, so it pays 0% US federal income tax — [Neo Expertise](https://neoexpertise.net/tax-morocco-foreign-investors/); [Businessanywhere](https://businessanywhere.io/non-us-residents-pay-us-taxes-on-us-llc-explained/)
- A dependent agent is a US person or company working primarily or exclusively for the LLC with authority to act for it, such as concluding contracts. This can create a USTB. A genuine independent contractor serving many clients generally does not — [Entity.inc](https://www.entity.inc/blog/effectively-connected-income-eci/); [Cheq Capital](https://cheqcapital.com/en/us-llc-tax-non-residents/)
- US inventory: one camp says inventory in a US warehouse "strongly indicates" a USTB and ECI, because sales are "considerable, continuous, and regular" and the seller owns the goods in the US. Another camp argues Amazon/3PL is an independent agent, so FBA alone may not create a USTB. No IRS ruling resolves the question — [James Baker CPA](https://jamesbakercpa.com/learn/ecommerce-sellers/); [Onlinetaxman](https://onlinetaxman.com/foreign-owned-llc-taxation); [James Baker CPA FBA guide](https://jamesbakercpa.com/blog/amazon-fba-us-tax-setup/)
- Treaty: signed Rabat Aug 1, 1977, in force Dec 30, 1981, general effect Jan 1, 1981. Article 4 = Permanent Establishment, Article 5 = Source of Income, Article 7 = Business Profits. The Treasury Technical Explanation describes it as similar to the OECD model with adjustments for Morocco as a developing country — [IRS Morocco Treasury explanation](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/morocco-treasury-explanation); [IRS treaty PDF](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/morocco.pdf)
- Article 4(1) defines PE as "a fixed place of business through which a resident of one of the Contracting States engages in industrial [or commercial activity]". The snippet was truncated — [IRS treaty PDF](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/morocco.pdf)
- Comparable Moroccan treaties of the same era exclude facilities used "solely for storage, display or delivery of goods" (Netherlands–Morocco 1977). They also exclude business done through a broker, general commission agent or independent agent acting in the ordinary course (Canada–Morocco). The US text was not verified — [Upsilon (NL–MA treaty)](https://upsilon-consulting.com/documents/morocco-netherlands-tax-treaty.pdf); [Canada–Morocco convention](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/tax-policy/tax-treaties/country/morocco-convention-1975.html)
- Independent personal services: Moroccan residents are taxed in the US only on income attributable to a US fixed base kept for more than 89 days (secondary summary) — [Legacy Tax](https://www.legacytaxresolutionservices.com/tax-treaties-between-the-u-s-and-morocco/)

### Inferences
- Current fact pattern (ships from Morocco, sells via own site/Etsy, no US people or premises): likely no USTB, so no ECI and no 1040-NR for the business income. This is the main "advantage": no US federal income tax. It is a consequence of the facts, not a loophole. Morocco still taxes the income (section 8).
- Moving stock to a US 3PL or warehouse to speed up shipping is the biggest decision that could change this. If a USTB exists, the treaty can still protect profits only if there is no PE. Where the treaty has a storage/delivery exclusion, a third-party 3PL may fit it. A treaty-based return position generally requires filing 1040-NR with Form 8833 (treaty disclosure). That is my knowledge of Reg. §301.6114-1, not sourced here, so verify with a CPA.
- A US-based VA with authority to accept orders or sign contracts, or a US "manager", could be a dependent agent. Keep any US contractors independent and non-exclusive, with no contracting authority.

### Gaps
- Full Article 4 and Article 7 text of the US–Morocco treaty (storage/delivery exclusion, dependent-agent clause, any limitation-on-benefits article) could not be read because irs.gov was blocked.
- No IRS ruling or case specific to non-resident e-commerce sellers using a 3PL.

## 3. Sales tax: nexus, marketplace facilitators, own-website sales, tools

### Takeaway
There is no federal sales tax. Each state's economic-nexus test (usually $100k in sales, some states also 200 transactions) applies to remote sellers, including foreign-owned LLCs. Etsy/Amazon, as marketplace facilitators, collect and remit on marketplace sales. Own-site (e.g., Shopify/Stripe) sales require registration only in states where the seller crosses a threshold, or where it has physical nexus such as US inventory. The trend is to drop the 200-transaction test.

### Cited Findings
- As of Aug 1 2026, 17+ states have eliminated the 200-transaction threshold. Illinois did so Jan 1 2026 and Kentucky Aug 1 2026. Earlier removals: SD, LA, IN, NC, WY, AK, UT (2023–2025) — [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/06/states-eliminating-economic-nexus-transaction-thresholds.html); [VATupdate Oct 2026](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/10/02/states-drop-200-transaction-sales-tax-nexus-thresholds/)
- Higher thresholds: California and Texas $500,000. New York requires more than $500,000 AND more than 100 sales over the preceding four quarters — [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/blog/how-to-determine-nexus-in-every-state); [James Baker CPA](https://jamesbakercpa.com/blog/economic-nexus-thresholds-by-state/)
- Whether marketplace sales count toward a seller's own threshold varies by state. Illinois, Alabama and Mississippi exclude them for sellers, while Hawaii includes them. Sources conflict on WA, MI and LA, so verify with the state DOR — [TaxCloud](https://taxcloud.com/blog/sales-tax-nexus-by-state/); [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/blog/economic-nexus-sales-tax)
- Stripe Tax: 0.5% per transaction (no-code) only where you are registered, and free threshold monitoring and alerts based on Stripe payments. Stripe does not register you by default. You register with each state, then add the registration in the dashboard. "Tax Complete" (registrations and filings) is a higher-priced plan — [Stripe Tax pricing](https://stripe.com/en-gr/tax/pricing); [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/how-tax-works)

### Inferences
- For a handmade seller below $100k of own-site sales to any single state, registration is likely needed nowhere, and Etsy handles marketplace sales. Watch CA/TX/NY (higher thresholds) and the states that count marketplace sales toward the seller's total.
- Stripe Tax monitoring (free) is the cheapest way to watch thresholds for own-site sales. TaxJar or Numeral are alternatives when filings must be automated. Resale certificates matter only if buying US inventory for resale. Handmade makers buying raw materials in Morocco do not need them.
- US inventory in a 3PL creates physical nexus in that state (and FBA in many states) regardless of thresholds.

### Gaps
- TaxJar and Numeral current pricing were not researched within the tool budget.
- State-by-state confirmation of the conflicting thresholds (WA, MI, LA) is needed.

## 4. BOI / Corporate Transparency Act status (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
A US-formed LLC is a "domestic" company, so under FinCEN's March 26 2025 interim final rule, which search results say was finalized effective Aug 14 2026, it is exempt from BOI reporting even if 100% foreign-owned. Only entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in a US state ("foreign reporting companies") must still report, and they report only non-US beneficial owners.

### Cited Findings
- The March 2025 interim final rule redefined "reporting company" to mean only entities formed under foreign law that registered to do business in a US state. Domestic companies and US persons are exempt — [Federal Register 2025-05199](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/26/2025-05199/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision-and-deadline-extension); [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/fincen-issues-interim-final-rule-removing-us-companies-and-persons-from-the-ctas-boi-reporting-requi)
- Change flag: FinCEN issued a final rule on Aug 11 2026, effective Aug 14 2026, making the narrowing permanent. It also removes the requirement for foreign companies to report US-person company applicants, and FinCEN will delete previously reported BOI about US persons — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/cta-beneficial-ownership-info-boi-reporting-fincen-final-rule.html); [Orrick, Sept 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/09/FinCEN-Permanently-Lifts-Certain-Beneficial-Ownership-Reporting-Requirements); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/fincen-narrows-beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirements/)

### Inferences
- The owner's LLC (formed in WY/NM/DE etc.) has no BOI filing obligation, regardless of the Moroccan owner's nationality. The "foreign" in "foreign reporting company" refers to the place of formation, not the owner. Ignore vendor upsells for "BOI filing".
- Earlier filings (if any were made in 2024) need no update.

### Gaps
- Could not read FinCEN.gov or the final-rule Federal Register text directly. The final-rule details rely on law-firm summaries (KPMG, Orrick, Wolters Kluwer) surfaced in search.

## 5. State annual reports, franchise taxes, registered agents, good standing

### Takeaway
Ongoing state costs are low in WY and NM and higher in DE and FL. The registered agent (required in every state) is typically the biggest recurring cost. Missing an annual report leads to administrative dissolution, which can cut off Stripe, bank and Etsy access.

### Cited Findings
- Wyoming: annual report due the 1st day of the anniversary month, $60 minimum (license tax based on WY assets) — [LLC University](https://www.llcuniversity.com/llc-annual-fees-by-state/); [Northwest Registered Agent](https://www.northwestregisteredagent.com/llc/state-annual-fees)
- New Mexico: no annual report and no annual fee for LLCs — [LLC University](https://www.llcuniversity.com/llc-annual-fees-by-state/)
- Delaware: annual LLC franchise tax $300, due June 1. Some copies of the table show $400, so verify with the DE Division of Corporations — [LLC University](https://www.llcuniversity.com/llc-annual-fees-by-state/)
- Florida: annual report $138.75, due May 1 (FL Dept. of State) — [LLC University](https://www.llcuniversity.com/llc-annual-fees-by-state/)
- Registered agent example: Firstbase charges $299/yr for registered agent service — [GlobalSolo cost index](https://www.globalsolo.global/data/llc-costs-by-state)

### Inferences
- Typical all-in state upkeep: NM about $0 + RA ($50–$300); WY about $60 + RA; DE about $300 + RA; FL $138.75 + RA. Late fees and reinstatement in any state cost more than staying current.
- Ensure the registered agent forwards IRS mail (CP notices about 5472 penalties arrive there or at the LLC's address of record).

### Gaps
- Official state SOS pages were not accessible to confirm 2026 fees. Delaware's $300 vs. $400 discrepancy is unresolved.

## 6. ITIN (Form W-7, CAA)

### Takeaway
The LLC uses an EIN. The Moroccan owner needs an ITIN only when a US tax filing or treaty claim requires it (e.g., filing 1040-NR, or some W-8BEN treaty claims). A Certifying Acceptance Agent (CAA) avoids mailing the original passport.

### Cited Findings
- A W-7 must be tied to a qualifying tax reason and is generally attached to a federal tax return (with listed exceptions). An ITIN is for tax purposes only: it gives no work authorization, immigration status or Social Security benefits — [Slash](https://www.slash.com/blog/itin-number); [Dimov Tax](https://dimovtax.com/form-w-7-itin-application/)
- A CAA verifies identity documents and certifies passport copies, so the original need not be mailed. Vendor-quoted CAA fees are $150–$400 — [Terms.law](https://terms.law/Invest-USA/entity-formation/itin-application.html); [Jupid](https://jupid.com/blog/what-is-an-itin-guide-2026)
- Processing times are quoted as 7–11 weeks, or 8–14 weeks as of May 2026 (sources disagree) — [Jupid](https://jupid.com/blog/what-is-an-itin-guide-2026); [file.business](https://design.file.business/blog/itin-application-form-w-7-complete-2026-guide)

### Inferences
- If the business stays non-ECI, an ITIN is optional. Practical benefits: some payment and banking providers and platforms prefer a personal TIN, it helps claim treaty rates on W-8BEN, and it is needed if a 1040-NR ever has to be filed (e.g., after moving inventory to the US). A stand-alone W-7 without a return is usually rejected unless an exception applies.

### Gaps
- Stripe, PayPal and Etsy's own ITIN/SSN requirements for non-resident LLC owners were not verifiable within budget.

## 7. 1099-K, W-8BEN / W-8BEN-E vs W-9, backup withholding

### Takeaway
Because the LLC is disregarded, the beneficial owner is the Moroccan individual. Per IRS instructions, the owner gives the payer a **W-8BEN** (individual), not a W-9 and not a W-8BEN-E for the LLC, and tells the payer the account is in the DE's name. Giving a W-9 wrongly represents US-person status and can lead to 1099-K/1099 reporting and mismatched records. 1099-K thresholds are back to more than $20,000 AND more than 200 transactions (OBBBA, retroactive).

### Cited Findings
- IRS W-8BEN-E instructions: "If you are a disregarded entity with a single owner ... the single owner, if such owner is a foreign person, should provide Form W-8BEN or Form W-8BEN-E (as appropriate). If the single owner is a U.S. person, a Form W-9 should be provided." The W-8BEN instructions add that the owner should inform the withholding agent if the account is in the disregarded entity's name — [IRS W-8BEN-E instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/iw8bene.pdf); [IRS W-8BEN instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben)
- Platforms sometimes ask foreign-owned LLCs for a W-9 (Stripe forum report). An Amazon forum reply recommends a W-9 for the LLC, which contradicts the IRS rule. A guide says Amazon's tax interview routes non-US owners to W-8BEN with the owner as beneficial owner — [Claimyr/Stripe thread](https://claimyr.com/government-services/irs/Stripe-Requesting-W9-Form-For-My-Foreign-Owned-LLC-Help-Me-Understand/2025-04-11); [Amazon Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/8d67965bc4305b52e48d321e8544232c); [Riverbend Consulting](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/amazon-tax-interview/)
- Without a valid W-8, payers may withhold 30% on US-source payments (vendor claim re Stripe) — [Monezzi](https://monezzi.com/w8ben-e)
- OBBBA restored the 1099-K threshold to more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions, retroactive to 2022. Stripe confirms it for the 2026 season. 1099-NEC/MISC and backup-withholding thresholds rise to $2,000 for 2026 — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/whats-new-for-the-2026-tax-season); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html); [Withum](https://www.withum.com/resources/big-relief-for-small-payments-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-changes/)

### Inferences
- Product-sale proceeds (Etsy, Stripe, PayPal) are not FDAP subject to 30% withholding. The W-8BEN mainly prevents 1099-K issuance and backup withholding (24%). Affiliate commissions (e.g., Amazon Associates) and ad revenue for services performed outside the US are generally foreign-source, so no US withholding applies with a proper W-8BEN. Some ad programs withhold on US-viewer revenue (YouTube is a known example), and the treaty rate depends on how the income is characterized.
- If a W-9 was already given with the LLC EIN, correct it to a W-8BEN. Otherwise IRS records will show 1099-Ks issued to a "US person" that files no 1040/1120 income, which can trigger notices.

### Gaps
- Etsy's, PayPal's and individual affiliate and ad networks' current form flows for foreign-owned DEs were not confirmed.
- The treaty rate for royalties/ad income under the US–Morocco treaty was not verified.

## 8. Morocco side: worldwide taxation, foreign-exchange (Office des Changes), auto-entrepreneur

### Takeaway
A Moroccan tax resident (more than 183 days in Morocco in any 12-month period) is taxed on worldwide income, so the disregarded LLC's profits are taxable in Morocco under progressive IR (0–37%). Foreign tax credits are available only for tax actually paid abroad, usually $0 here. Foreign-exchange control is the bigger trap: holding assets or accounts abroad requires authorization, and income from online services rendered from Morocco must be repatriated within 90 days (circular 3/2020).

### Cited Findings
- Residents are taxed on worldwide income, non-residents only on Moroccan-source income. Residency = more than 183 days in Morocco in any 12-month period. Foreign tax credit is capped at the Moroccan tax due. PIT is progressive 0–37% across six brackets (Finance Law 2025) — [Neo Expertise](https://neoexpertise.net/tax-morocco-foreign-investors/); [CMS](https://cms.law/fr/mar/news-information/obligations-declaratives-pour-les-residents-fiscaux-du-maroc); [Lembra Conseil](https://lembra-conseil.com/residence-fiscale-maroc-mre-resident-non-resident/)
- US LLC income remains taxable in Morocco under the progressive IR scale (worldwide principle) — [creer-llc-usa.com](https://creer-llc-usa.com/articles/fr/llc-usa-maroc-2026)
- Change flag: one firm says Finance Law 2026 introduced a specific annual declaration of foreign-source income, due before April 1 of the following year. Another source says March 1. Single-source and conflicting, so verify with the DGI — [CMS](https://cms.law/fr/mar/news-information/obligations-declaratives-pour-les-residents-fiscaux-du-maroc); [Cabinet AOS](https://cabinet-aos.com/blog/fiscalite/residence-fiscale-maroc-consequences/)
- Office des Changes General Instruction (IGOC, 2 Jan 2024): opening accounts abroad and holding assets abroad by resident Moroccan individuals requires authorization unless a general or specific authorization applies. It also provides foreign-currency accounts for resident individuals not in the commercial register who have foreign-source income — [IGOC 2024 (oc.gov.ma)](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/reglementation/pdf/2024-01/IGOC%202024.pdf)
- Remuneration for online services rendered by Moroccan residents must be repatriated within 90 days of the service date (Office des Changes DG, Jan 2022, under circular 3/2020) — [La Vie Éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/au-royaume/services-rendus-en-ligne-le-rapatriement-des-remunerations-doit-etre-realise-dans-un-delai-de-90-jours/); [Yabiladi](https://www.yabiladi.com/articles/details/123276/maroc-rapatriement-remunerations-services-rendus.html); [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/office-des-changes-le-rapatriement-des-remunerations-doit-etre-realise-dans-un-delai-de-90-jours)
- The older (2013) instruction requires resident Moroccan nationals to declare all foreign assets to the Office des Changes (1959 decree basis) — [2013 IGOC](https://www.finances.gov.ma/Publication/oc/2013/5431_instruction_generale_change.pdf)
- Auto-entrepreneur status (with ICE/IF identifiers, CNSS) is the common Moroccan vehicle for freelancers — [Omnivoo](https://omnivoo.com/blog/paying-moroccan-contractors-from-us-company); [Mellow](https://help.mellow.io/en/articles/10110008-freelance-and-taxes-morocco)

### Inferences
- Holding a US LLC with a US bank account (Mercury/Wise/Payoneer) is, for a Moroccan resident, technically an asset or account abroad. Exchange-control exposure (unauthorized foreign holdings, failure to repatriate) is a real risk that US-centric LLC vendors rarely mention. A common structure is to bill or sell through the LLC and regularly repatriate profits to a Moroccan account. The owner declares the income in Morocco, as auto-entrepreneur or under the professional income regime, which needs Moroccan advice. The "0% US tax" usually means the full Moroccan rate applies.
- Note the irony: no US tax paid means no foreign tax credit in Morocco.

### Gaps
- Whether the auto-entrepreneur regime (and its turnover caps) can legally cover income earned via a foreign LLC was not established.
- No official Office des Changes text explicitly addressing a resident owning a foreign company (US LLC) was found. A Moroccan lawyer/fiduciary is needed.

## 9. Deductible expenses, bookkeeping tools, specialist providers and costs

### Takeaway
With no ECI, US deductions are irrelevant to US tax, but clean books are still needed for the 5472 (related-party transactions) and for Moroccan tax, where deductions do matter. Bundled providers charge about $1,000–$2,000+/yr. A standalone CPA for 1120/5472 costs about $500–$2,000/yr.

### Cited Findings

## 10. Pitfalls & horror stories (Reddit and practitioner reports)

### Takeaway
Search could not surface Reddit threads (the site: operator was not honored), so the documented pitfalls come from practitioner blogs. The recurring disaster is the missed 5472 bringing an automatic $25k notice (CP15-type). First-time abatement is reportedly unavailable, so relief depends on a reasonable-cause argument via Form 843 or a response to the notice.

### Cited Findings
- First-time abatement: conflicting. Practitioner sites say the IRM excludes FTA for penalties tied to returns attached to another return (incl. 5472). An IRM excerpt appeared to list 5472 under FTA but was truncated. Verify the current IRM 20.1.1 — [IRM 20.1.1](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part20/irm_20-001-001r); [Optimize Tax](https://optimizetax.io/blog/form-5472-penalty-relief/); [Blue Cloud CPA](https://bluecloudcpa.com/guides/form-5471-5472-penalty-abatement-tax-court)
- Reasonable cause is decided on facts and circumstances. "First-time failure" alone is insufficient. Reliance on a competent professional can qualify if they had full information. For assessed penalties, file Form 843 with a reasonable-cause statement, and do not simply pay without requesting abatement — [Taxes for Expats](https://www.taxesforexpats.com/articles/expat-tax-rules/reasonable-cause-penalty-abatement-relief.html); [SDO CPA](https://www.sdocpa.com/form-5472-penalties-relief-guide/)
- Vendor "horror" scenarios (e.g., "$50,000+ accumulated by 2026") are hypothetical calculations, not documented cases — [GlobalSolo](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/what-happens-if-you-miss-form-5472-non-resident-llc)

### Inferences
- The top pitfalls for this owner:
  1. Missing the 5472/1120 in a zero-activity or first year.
  2. Giving W-9s to platforms.
  3. Ignoring Moroccan tax and exchange control because "the US says 0%".
  4. Moving inventory to a US 3PL without re-analyzing USTB, nexus and treaty position.
  5. Letting the state annual report or registered agent lapse.
  6. Paying for unnecessary BOI filings.
- For a late 5472 filed before any IRS notice, practitioners typically file immediately with a reasonable-cause statement attached.
